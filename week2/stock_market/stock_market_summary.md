# Stock Market Fundamentals — India

## 1. What is the Stock Market?

The stock market is a place where people buy and sell shares of companies. Companies sell shares to raise money for their business. Investors may earn money when share prices increase or when companies pay dividends. However, losses are also possible.

## 2. Indian Stock Exchanges and Indices

- **NSE:** National Stock Exchange of India, where people buy and sell shares.
- **BSE:** BSE Ltd., formerly known as Bombay Stock Exchange, is another major stock exchange in India.
- **Nifty 50:** An index that tracks 50 selected companies listed on NSE.
- **Sensex:** An index that tracks 30 selected companies listed on BSE.

Nifty and Sensex help us understand whether the overall stock market is rising or falling.

## 3. IPO, SME IPO and Market Capitalization

- **IPO (Initial Public Offering):** When a company offers its shares to the public for the first time.
- **SME IPO:** An IPO for a small or medium-sized company through a stock exchange's SME platform.
- **Market Capitalization:** The total market value of a company's outstanding shares.

**Formula:** Market Capitalization = Current Share Price × Total Outstanding Shares

Example: If a company has 1 lakh shares and each share costs ₹100, its market capitalization is ₹1 crore.

## 4. Important Financial Terms

- **P/E Ratio:** Shows how much investors pay for every ₹1 of a company's earnings per share.
- **P/B Ratio:** Compares a company's share price with its book value per share.
- **EPS (Earnings Per Share):** Shows how much profit is available for each equity share.
- **Dividend:** Money a company distributes to its shareholders when a dividend is declared.
- **Bonus Shares:** Extra shares given to existing shareholders without additional payment.
- **Stock Split:** When a company divides its existing shares into more shares, usually at a lower face value per share.
- **Trading Volume:** The total number of shares traded during a particular period.

Bonus shares and stock splits increase the number of shares held, but they do not automatically increase the total value of an investor's investment.

## 5. Company Financial Statements

- **Balance Sheet:** Shows what a company owns (assets), what it owes (liabilities), and the owners' share (equity) on a particular date.

  **Formula:** Assets = Liabilities + Equity

- **Profit and Loss Statement:** Shows the company's income, expenses and profit or loss over a period.

- **Cash Flow Statement:** Shows how cash comes into and goes out of a company through operating, investing and financing activities.

## 6. Basic Financial Ratios

- **Revenue Growth:** Shows how much a company's sales have increased or decreased compared with the previous period.

- **Net Profit Margin:** Shows how much profit a company earns from its revenue.

  **Formula:** Net Profit Margin = (Net Profit ÷ Revenue) × 100

- **Current Ratio:** Compares current assets with current liabilities to understand whether a company can pay its short-term dues.

  **Formula:** Current Ratio = Current Assets ÷ Current Liabilities

- **Debt-to-Equity Ratio:** Compares a company's debt with its shareholders' equity to understand how much it depends on borrowing.

  **Formula:** Debt-to-Equity Ratio = Total Debt ÷ Shareholders' Equity

## Conclusion

Stock market knowledge helps us understand how companies raise money and how their shares are traded. Financial statements and ratios help analysts study a company's sales, profits, cash flow and financial position. These concepts are useful for understanding financial data and business performance.
